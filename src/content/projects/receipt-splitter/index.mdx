## The story

Every group dinner ends the same way: one person holding the receipt, reading line items
out loud, while everyone does phone math. I wanted the lazy version, take a photo, say
who had what, get the split.

So I built it. The computer reads the text off the receipt photo, then an AI does the
assigning and the arithmetic, tax and tip split fairly. What actually broke, over and
over, wasn't the AI: it was the receipts. Thermal paper fades, people fold them, a
coffee ring turns an 8 into a 3. The unglamorous step before the clever step is where
these things really fail, a lesson I've reused constantly since.

The other keeper: a confident wrong answer is worse than an error message. Today I'd
make it re-add the split and refuse to show one that doesn't match the printed total.

</PlainOnly>

## What it does

Point it at a receipt photo. Tesseract (through Tess4J) extracts the text. The text goes to
an OpenAI chat completion with an instruction to split the items between the named people
and return the totals, including tax and tip proportionally. A console program, deliberately, the point was the pipeline, not the UI.

## Why OCR first

The obvious build is to hand the photo straight to a multimodal model and ask for the split.
I didn't, for two reasons. The model was the expensive and least-predictable step, so I
wanted it to do as little as possible. And I wanted a text intermediate I could read, when
the split was wrong, I could see whether the OCR misread a price or the model mis-assigned
an item, instead of staring at one opaque answer.

## What broke

Not the model. The receipts. Thermal paper fades, people fold them, and a coffee ring turns
an 8 into a 3. Most of the effort went into image preprocessing before Tesseract saw it. The
lesson stuck: when a feature has an AI step, the unglamorous input step is where it actually
fails.

## What I'd do now

Validate the output against the printed total and refuse to show a split that doesn't add up.
Keep the OCR-first shape. And wrap it in something my friends would actually open at the
table, which is a different project.

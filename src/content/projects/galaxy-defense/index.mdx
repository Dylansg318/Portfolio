I made this in my senior year of high school, in Code.org's Game Lab. You build by
dragging blocks. The blocks generate JavaScript. It's designed so that a person who
has never written code can finish something in an afternoon.

It isn't designed for what I did with it.

<Demo demo={frontmatter.demo} title={frontmatter.title} />

## What the constraints actually cost

Game Lab gives you global variables and a `draw()` loop that runs every frame. It does
not give you arrays you can iterate, classes, or any collection type. So there's no
way to say "for each alien, check whether it hit a projectile." You write it out.

Five aliens times three projectiles is fifteen collision checks, each four lines long,
all identical apart from two identifiers:

The same pressure shows up in the bases. Each of the three bases has eight damage
states, and the only way to select one is a comparison per state, twenty-four
if-statements to express what a single array index would say today:

<CodeFile src="galaxy-defense/snippets/original.js" lines="628-652" title="Eight damage states, one if-statement each" />

That's the whole lesson of the project, and I didn't understand it at the time. The
repetition isn't bad style. It's the *only* style available. What a loop and a
collection buy you isn't elegance, it's the ability to add a sixth alien without
touching fifteen places.

## The parts I'm still glad I built

**The shop.** Hold `T` and the field freezes into a storefront: bombs for 20 points,
a life for 50, a full repair of all three bases for 200. Adding prices changed what
the score *was*. Before the shop it was a number that went up. After it, every kill
was twenty-something percent of a bomb, and dying with 190 unspent points was a
genuine mistake.

**The FTL drive.** A gauge that fills one percent per frame. At 100 you can press `Q`
with a direction and jump a hundred pixels. It's an escape ability with a cooldown
expressed as a resource, and it made the ship feel like it had a gear the aliens
didn't.

**The boss.** At 30 points an angry alien enters and bounces across the screen taking
two hits instead of one, and it takes two health off a base instead of one. One
sprite, a velocity flip at each wall, and a second health variable give you a difficulty
curve out of almost nothing.

## The menus aren't a pause. There's no pause.

This is my favourite thing in the file, and I didn't design it. It fell out of
what the environment refused to give me.

Game Lab has no way to stop the draw loop. It runs every frame, forever. So when I
wanted a help card and a shop, the only move available was to put the world back
where it started on every single pass while the key is held:

<CodeFile src="galaxy-defense/snippets/original.js" lines="145-167" title="The store &quot;pausing&quot; the game by re-parking every sprite, every frame" />

It looks frozen. Nothing stopped. And once you notice that, four things follow that
I never wrote down anywhere:

**The shop is a panic button.** Every alien is teleported to the top of the screen
each frame the menu is open. About to be hit? Hold `T`. The thing about to hit you is
now four hundred pixels away.

**The menus are the only respawn in the game.** Nothing resets an alien that falls
off the bottom of the screen. I never wrote that check. The bases only cover part of
the width, so any alien that drops through a gap is gone permanently. Play without
ever opening a menu and the game slowly empties out. Hold `T` for one frame and all
five are back. The pause I built by accident is also the garbage collector I never
built on purpose.

**The FTL drive charges while you shop.** The movement function runs before the
store's reset and doesn't care whether a menu is open, so the gauge fills the whole
time. The shop is a safe place to refuel.

**It costs you your position.** The same reset that saves you also snaps your ship
back to the centre of the screen and throws the bomb pickup you were three pixels
from collecting back above the top of the map.

None of that is in the help text, because none of it was intentional. It's what a
"pause" becomes when the only tool you have is *assignment*, executed thirty times a
second. I ported it exactly as written, phase order and all, because a version
that used a real pause would be a different game.

## Porting it here, six years later

The version above is the original logic on a plain canvas, so it runs on this page
without Code.org's runtime. Field size, speeds, spawn ranges, prices, health totals
and the FTL charge rate are all the original's numbers.

Reading my own code back, three things stood out.

**A real bug, now fixed.** At `baseHealth2 === 7` the original sets the damage sprite
on `homeBase` instead of `homeBase2`, so taking the first hit on the middle base
visibly changed the wrong one. It survived because both bases look identical at a
glance and the state only lasts until the next hit.

**A typo I kept.** The teleport spends 98 charge going right and 99 in every other
direction. Jumping right is very slightly cheaper than jumping left, forever. It
changes nothing, nobody would notice, and removing it would make the port less true
to the thing I actually shipped.

**A change I made on purpose.** The original redraws its starfield by calling
`ellipse()` at eighteen random positions *every frame*, so the stars strobe at 60fps.
On a school assignment that's a quirk. On a public page it's a photosensitivity risk,
so the field is generated once and held.

**One more, found while wiring the art up.** Game Lab runs `draw()` at 30fps, and
every speed in the original is written per frame. The ship moves "4", the FTL drive
charges "1". Dropping that straight into a 60Hz animation loop silently doubles the
speed of the entire game. The port steps its simulation on a fixed 30Hz clock and
only draws on the display's schedule, so the ship still crosses the screen in the
time it used to.

And a small mercy: p5.play collides on a sprite's whole image box, transparent margin
included, so an alien's real hitbox was the full 50×50 canvas around a 31×19 drawing.
Shots that visibly missed still counted. Collisions here use the visible art.

The sprites are the ones I drew in Piskel and imported into Code.org, the same
files, cropped to their artwork and re-hosted. The aliens still have their two-frame
walk, the bomb still spins through six.
